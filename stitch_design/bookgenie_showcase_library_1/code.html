T he silence at the galactic periphery was not an absence of noise, but an impenetrable density of dead transmitters. The star system before them was unnamed on imperial charts—a pale amber dwarf dying in recursive gravitational flares.

“Every gate in this sector was burned down forty cycles before the schism,” she noted into the slate microphone, her voice flat, practiced in the mechanical cadence of deep-range surveyors. “Yet the interference pattern in the chromosphere holds an uncompressed eight-beat cadence. She adjusted the mechanical micrometer, locking the aperture at 540 nanometers. Through the viewport, the void flared in deep cyan luminescence—the exact wavelength of artificial resonance crystals.
