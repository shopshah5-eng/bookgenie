The Glass Pavilion

I t was not merely the cold salt that permeated the glass ribs of the conservatory, but a stillness so total that even the clockwork pendulum seemed to pause mid-stroke. Julian traced the brass armature with a thumb calloused by months at sea. Across the estuary, the kerosene beacons flared in rhythmic sequence, piercing the slate fog like needlework upon dark wool.

Active Block • Rev. Draft

He had calculated the azimuth twice before dusk, yet the pendulum continued its irregular sweep. Within the bronze astrolabe, a grain of Baltic sand had lodged itself between the lunar gear and the outer meridian ring, murmuring with each minute rotation like a held breath.

Outside, the tide claimed the lowest stone risers of the terrace. He knew that by midnight, the seawall would be entirely submerged, leaving only the copper cupola suspended like a lantern above the ocean.

†

The meridian clock of Saint-Germain was engineered by V. Delaunay in 1842; its chronometric error was recorded at less than two seconds per lunar cycle.
